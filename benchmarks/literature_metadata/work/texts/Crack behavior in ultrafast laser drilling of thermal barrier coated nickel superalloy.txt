Clare Keywords: Laser drilling Ultrafast laser Crack behavior Thermal stress Phase transformation stress TBC A B S T R A C T Quantitative analysis is required to explore the mechanism of crack generation in ultrafast laser drilling of thermal barrier coated nickel superalloy. In this study, a simple thermo-mechanical coupled model is established to obtain temperature history, phase transformation stress and thermal stress during drilling process, in which laser beam scanning, laser intensity attenuation, the nonlinear relationship between drilling depth and drilling time are involved. The induced stress obtained from the present model is compared with the results by digital image correlation (DIC) method. According to the crack distribution around the drilled hole, the present model is used to explain the mechanism of crack behavior in drilling of multilayer materials. Finally, the strategy using low pulse repetition rate or water jet assisted method is suggested to reduce the thermal effect in ultrafast laser drilling process. 1. Introduction In order to improve turbine performance, heat insulation and cooling techniques are used for turbine blades, in which the ceramic layer with low thermal conductivity is adopted for heat insulation of blades and the film cooling technology is to utilize cold air through the gas passage to isolate the blade from the high temperature gas. Padture et al. (2002) demonstrates that the application of both thermal barrier coating (TBC) and film cooling technology allows the engine to operate at temperature over the melting temperature of the substrate. A typical TBC system consists of a TBC layer, a bond coat (BC) layer and a substrate. Traditional methods for drilling film holes in this material system include laser drilling, electric discharge machining, electro stream machining and so on. Among them, laser drilling be- comes more popular due to its flexibility, but this removal process produces significant thermal effects, resulting in defects such as dela- mination cracks and the recast layer (Beck, 2011). Ultrafast laser dril- ling, typically with the pulse duration of less than 10 ps, can complete energy deposition before the material achieves local heat equilibrium, in which the heat affected zone (HAZ), thermal deformation, stress, cracks and recast layer are effectively reduced (Schulz et al., 2013). Therefore, ultrafast laser provides the possibility to attain high hole quality in the TBC by the vaporization dominated process (Dorsch et al., 2016) and relatively less heat accumulation (Klotzbach et al., 2016). The theoretical and experimental studies mainly involve only sev- eral pulses in ultrafast laser drilling, however high repetition rate is normally used to improve the processing efficiency (Taras et al., 2018), which results in heat accumulation even with the help of ultrafast laser (Finger and Reininghaus, 2014), leading to drilling defects such as deposits (Das and Pollock, 2009) and cracks (Zhang et al., 2017). Cracks in stainless steel (Mincuzzi et al., 2019), single-crystal diamond (Hsu et al., 2011), glass (Ali and Sundaram, 2018), fused silica (Wang et al., 2017) and ceramic (Feng and Shen, 2019) by ultrafast laser have been investigated. For ultrafast laser drilling of superalloy with TBC, the computational fluid dynamics method was used to simulate the interfacial cracks (Fan et al., 2016). Yilbas et al. (2011) found the micro cracks formed on the surface due to the high stress levels around the hole. The repetition rate was the most significant parameter for heat accumulation in ultrafast laser drilling (Sun et al., 2019). In summary, most studies on crack behavior in ultrafast laser dril- ling are qualitative analysis due to the challenges in modelling of thousands of ultrafast laser pulses in drilling process. In this study, crack behavior around the hole trepanned by a picosecond pulse laser is quantitatively analyzed. A numerical model considering the temporal and spatial characteristics of laser pulse is established to obtain the temperature during drilling process. Experiment procedures 2.1. Materials The multilayer material with the size of 50 mm × 50 mm × 1.35 mm includes three layers: TBC layer, BC layer and substrate. The TBC layer with the thickness of 400 μm deposited by air plasma spraying (APS) consists of 8 wt% yttria stabilized zirconia (YSZ) with Metco 204B-NS. The BC layer is NiCoCrAlY alloy (Amdry 365-2) and has a jagged rough upper surface (Fig. 1). Commercially available nickel-base superalloy (GH3536) with the thickness of 750 μm is used as the substrate. Chemical compositions of the BC layer and the substrate are shown in Table 1. 2.2. Experimental setup The laser trepanning process was accomplished by a pulsed laser (TruMicro 5050) with a wavelength of 1030 nm. The laser was operated at 8 ps pulse duration with maximum pulse energy of 100 μJ, Rayleigh length of 0.6 mm and basic repetition rate of 400 kHz. The experimental setup of laser drilling system is depicted in Fig. 2, including a laser generator and an optical system delivering the laser beam. Through the galvanometric scanning system, the laser beam is focused by a lens with a focal length of 100 mm, to provide a spot diameter of approximately 20.4 μm. During the drilling process, the focal plane was maintained at the upper surface of the sample and the laser beam was incident perpen- dicularly to the sample surface. The hole diameter is approximately 400 µm. Laser parameters used in the drilling process are listed in Table 2. 2.3. Hole quality evaluation In order to identify the relationship between drilling depth and drilling time, the industrial computed tomography (CT) instrument (XTH 225 st, Nikon) was used to obtain the drilling depth at different time. The scanning step was set to 8 μm and the focal spot diameter was 3 μm, achieving a feature recognition resolution of 1 μm. After cleaned in an ultrasonic machine, the drilled samples were dealt with subsequent cutting, grinding and polishing to acquire ob- servable sectioned pieces. Then the section morphology of holes was obtained by a metallurgical microscope and the detailed features of cracks were further recorded through scanning electron microscope (SEM). The tetragonal phase of YSZ can exist in ambient temperature be- cause of the stabilization by yttria, but the tetragonal phase transforms to monoclinic phase (t-m phase transformation) during the cooling period in the region where the peak temperature is over 1423 K ac- companied with volume expansion (Lughi and Clarke, 2007). Raman spectroscopy (Lab RAM HR 800, = 532 nm) was used to detect the t- m phase transformation around the drilled hole. A measurement step of 20 μm was adopted near the hole edge within 600 μm (Fig. 3a) on the upper surface. Meanwhile, some points were selected on the upper surface (Fig. 3b) and cross section of the sample (Fig. 3c), respectively. Numerical model The thermo-mechanical coupled simulation for the ultrafast laser drilling process is difficult, because the physical process is complex in drilling of the TBC system, involving a series of phenomena such as laser rotary cutting, material heating, material removing, laser-material action point moving, laser energy attenuating, thermal stress and phase transformation. In this study, the influence of heat accumulation on the drilling defects is mainly concerned, thus the complicated physical process can be rationally simplified. Some assumptions are listed as follows: (1) Heat source. The laser energy distribution is assumed as a typical Gaussian distribution. (2) Geometric features. All surfaces and the BC/substrate interface are considered as planes, and the TBC/BC interface is set as a serrated structure. (3) Material properties. Material at each layer is considered to be homogenous and isotropic. (4) Material removal. Materials where the temperature is higher than the boiling point are removed. (5) Drilling process. Melting, recoil pressure and the absorption of laser by the vapor and plasma are ignored. (6) Phase transformation. The t-m phase transformation is considered, and the reverse phase transformation and the tetragonal to cubic phase transformation are ignored. (7) Thermal stress. The elastic model is used to calculate the thermal stress, ignoring the plastic effect. 3.1.
