Introduction With the increase of the turbine inlet temperature of high performance aero-engine day by day, high-temperature strength and service life of the turbine blades are becoming more and more demanded [1–6]. Turbine blade material has developed from equiaxial crystal cast superalloy to directionally solidiﬁed superalloy and single crystal superalloy. DZ125 has been widely used in turbine rotor blades due to its excellent high temperature mechanical properties. However, the existence of hollows and holes, causing stress concentration, complex stress condition and microcracks, plays an important role on fatigue processes. Therefore, the effects of drilling processes [7–10], interference [11], hole dimension [12], nucleation mechanisms [13–15], temper- ature gradient [16], cycle/dwelling time [17–18] and tension-compression asymmetry [19] on the fatigue-creep characteristics and life prediction near the hole and notch are highly concerned at all times. Thus, studying the effect of picosecond laser and nano- second laser drilling on the hole design and life evaluation of the blade, including single-hole and multi-hole specimens, has a great signiﬁcance. In this paper, the LCF tests were carried out on DZ125 at 900 °C. The effect of drilling process and the number of holes on LCF tests were examined. The fracture morphologies were observed by SEM. [PAGE 2] 2. Materials and experiment details The experiment were conducted at 900 °C using directionally solidiﬁed superalloy DZ125, referring to Table 1 for its chemical composition [20]. In order to simplify the study, experiments were carried out on thin-wall plate specimens with single hole and multi holes to model the cooling holes. Fig. 1 is the sample of the plate specimens. Respectively, picosecond and nanosecond laser drilling are chosen as the production process. All fatigue tests were conducted on a servo-hydraulic material testing system (Instron8802) at 900 °C. This temperature was limited in ±5 °C. Trapezoidal load waveforms, which was described in Fig. 2, were used in fatigue tests. The stress ratio R of min- imum to maximum stress is 0.1 and the loading frequency f = 3 Hz. The average stress on the minimum cross section that per- pendicular to the loading direction is 415 MPa. All the test data were recorded by the computer automatically. And the fracture morphology was observed by scanning electron microscope (SEM). 3. Results and discussion 3.1. The experiment results Twenty specimens were employed in this test. The results of DZ125 with ﬁlm cooling holes are given in Table 2. These results indicate that, under the 415 MPa-stress-controlled, fatigue life of DZ125 differs signiﬁcantly under different processes of laser dril- ling. For the single-holed specimens, the fatigue life of picosecond laser specimens (the average life is 47.61 h) have greatly im- proved, compared to that of nanosecond laser specimens (the average life is 21.18 h). The former is 2.25 times that of the latter. As for the multi-holed specimens, the fatigue life of picosecond laser specimens (the average life is 15.57 h) N nanosecond laser specimens (the average life is 6.37 h). The former is 2.44 times of the latter. It can be found that, under the process of picosecond laser, the average fatigue life of single-holed specimens is 3.06 times that of multi-holed ones. While under the process of nano- second laser, the life of single-holed specimens is 3.32 times that of multi-holed specimens. We can conclude that because of the existence of ﬁlm cooling holes, which leads to the stress redistribution, the material around the ﬁlm holes are in the multiaxial stress state. In addition, the stress interference effect among the cooling holes makes the stress distribution more complex. As to the dispersion, the dispersion of single-holed specimens is obviously higher than that of multi-holed specimens, as shown in Fig. 3. 3.2. Fatigue fracture analysis 3.2.1. [PAGE 3] Due to the difference of the process of laser drilling, the aperture of the specimen is not the same. Compared with the pico- second laser drilling process, the aperture drilled by nanosecond laser is uneven, trumpet-shaped, along the thickness direction. The characteristics of macroscopic fracture morphology under picosecond laser and nanosecond laser hole process are basically the same: the fracture appears gray-green on the whole, and the color of fatigue source zone is lighter, looking gray. There are three obvious characteristic zones: fatigue source zone, fatigue crack propagation zone and fatigue fracture zone, which are mostly perpendicular to the loading direction. Fracture reveals multi-source cracking characteristics on the macro, which originate from ﬁlm cooling hole. Under the same stress condition, the area of fatigue crack propagation zone with nanosecond laser drilling pro- cess is smaller and cone-shaped. The microstructure of fatigue crack propagation zone revealed that, the radial-type ridge centering on the fatigue source zone is more apparent and uniform in picosecond laser drilling specimens. However, in the nanosecond laser drilling specimens, the radial-type ridge is biased toward large-aperture side. The oxidation in fatigue zone is serious, no fatigue striation can be found. Scanning electron microscope (SEM) analyses of fatigue fracture zone indicate that there exists many cleavage-like facets in the high temperature fracture area. And fatigue fracture zone shows the dendrite fracture characteristics. Thus it can be seen that, under high temperature, nucleation initiates from the center in micro-void-coalescenced way. Then, gradually micro-voids connect together and micro-void coalescence fracture occurs. Due to the good plasticity of DZ125, the stress states change, transforming from plane strain state to plane stress state, when the experiment progresses to a certain stage, which leads to shear-mode fracture. Because of its own characteristics of the directionally solidiﬁed superalloy, the shear-mode fracture proceeds along a speciﬁc crystallographic plane, which leads to the existence of offset angle at the edge of the fracture. There exists the differences of the fatigue fracture characteristics under nanosecond laser drilling and picosecond laser drilling process. The LCF test of multi-holed specimens Generally, the fracture reveals that the crack is initiated from the internal holes. The specimen fractures immediately once the main crack propagates to the surface, in Figs. 6(a) and 7(a). From Figs. 6(b) to 7(b), it can be seen that the area between the mid- dle hole and fringe hole is relatively ﬂat, where lies the steady propagation zone. Therefore, there are stress concentrations around the holes. And the fracture initiates from the high stress area. When the fatigue crack propagates to the low stress area, the spec- imen fails. We can't ﬁnd any evident dimple over the whole fracture. Around the cooling holes, there are micro-voids, these are defects generated by the casting operation, which provide conditions for crack initiation and propagation. In addition, oxidation is observed at the crack initiation location, which might be a reason for crack nucleation and propagation. The specimen fractures when the crack extends to the edge of the specimen. Thus the fracture is rough. Similar to single-holed specimens, the microscopic observation of crack propagation zone shows that the radial-type ridge cen- tered on fatigue source zone is more apparent and uniform in picosecond laser drilling specimens. On the other hand, the radial- type ridge is biased toward large-aperture side with nanosecond laser drilling. The oxidation in fatigue zone is severe and no fa- tigue striation can be found. According to the fracture morphology, the crack initiates from the large-aperture side. Furthermore, deformation and damage deﬂect to the large-aperture side. 4.
