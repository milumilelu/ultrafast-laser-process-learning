Варьируя энергию пикосе- кундных импульсов Nd:YAG-лазера на 4-й гармонике 266 нм в пределах 1-9 мДж и чис- ло импульсов, сверлили линейки и решетки сквозных отверстий диаметром от 9 до 40 µм на площади до 1×1 cм2. При плотности энергии сфокусированного импульса >108 Дж/м2 вокруг этих отверстий генерировались ударные волны 4-5 µм от расширяющейся плазмы и происходила наноразмерная кристаллизация. Также в стороне от центрального микронного отверстия образуется субмикронная дырка за счет генерации вакуумных УФ-гармоник. Комбинированное воздействие γ-облучения с последующим сверлением УФ-лазером привело к уменьшению содержания кремния и кислорода и образованию фтора, что может быть вызвано фотоядерными реакциями 28Si14→19F9+33Т1; 16O8→44He2; 16О8+3Т1→19F9. Из-за вязкости стекла и релаксации в течение 2 месяцев при 300 К вы- ходной диаметр просверленных отверстий увеличился до размера на входе. Такие мик- рорешетки могут быть использованы для сепарации жидких смесей или очистки воды. Ключевые слова: гамма-индуцированное окрашивание, лазерное сверление стекла, ударная вол- на плазмы, фотоядерная реакция. The low efficiency of UV-laser ablation of transparent alkali-silicate glass has been increased by means of irradiation by 60Co γ-quanta to doses 104−107 Gy to induce intensive UV-visible coloration. Varying the picosecond pulse energy of Nd:YAG laser at 4-harmonics 266 nm within 1−9 mJ and pulse number, arrays and grids of holes were drilled with diameters from 9 to 40 µm on area up to 1×1 cm2. Shock waves of 4−5 µm were generated by expanding plasma around ablated holes at focused pulse energy density >108 J/m2 and nanoscale crystallization occurred there. Besides, a submicron hole was drilled aside the central micron hole due to gen- eration of vacuum UV harmonics. The combined effect of γ-irradiation followed by UV laser drilling has resulted in decrease of silicon and oxygen contents, and generation of fluorine that may be caused by photonuclear reactions 28Si14→19F9+ 33Т1; 16O8→44He2; 16О8+3Т1→19F9. Because of glass viscosity and relaxation during 2 months at 300 K, the exit diameter of drilled holes has increased up to the entry one. Such micro-scale grids can be used for separating liq- uid mixtures or cleaning water. Introduction The earliest report on nanosecond and femtose- cond excimer laser ablation of fused silica under con- dition of intensive UV-absorption was in 1992 [1].

Nazarov ©Academy of Sciences of Republic of Uzbekistan, 2022 Then femtosecond (fs) optical breakdown in dielec- trics was discussed in [2]. Some years later commer- cial applications of micromachining of fused silica with 1 picosecond laser were reported in [3]. Particu- lar morphology of surfaces of borosilicate glass and other transparent materials ablated with fs-laser was found in [4-6]. Then nanogratings were fabricated in fused silica with the morphology depending on fs- pulse duration [7]. The fs-laser induced changes in the refraction index n of boron-silica, alumina-silica and glasses doped with heavy metal oxides were ex- amined in details in [8]. One should mention that lo- cal ∆n=0.01 is enough for application in 3D-optical memory of high density. The laser-induced near- subwavelength ripples were attributed to interference between surface plasmons and incident laser wave [9]. Femtosecond laser-induced periodic surface struc- tures were found in [10]. Micro-holes were drilled in silicon above the forbidden gap Eg with 355 nm pico- second and nanosecond pulses, and threshold for plasma generation was about 3×108 Wt/cm2 [11]. Generation of higher harmonics due to non-linear processes at high power density conditions was con- sidered in details in the review [12]. Ultrafast laser pulses of a µJ energy tightly focused inside the bulk of a transparent solid have the MJ/cm3 energy density within a micro-scale focal volume significantly above the threshold for optical breakdown and material ab- lation, and are used for micro-patterning (grooves) of surface of glass substrates [13]. UV fs-lasers are used also for extruding glass fibers of nano- and submi- cron diameters [14]. Excimer laser (193 nm) illumi- nation through a mask allows parallel drilling of 1000 through holes in 30−100 µm thin glass interposers for 2.5D and 3D packaging small consumer electronics [15]. Multilayer dielectric gratings were made with picosecond laser in vacuum [16]. Few-cycle pulse la- ser induced damage threshold was determined in ul- tra-broadband optics [17]. Collimated relativistic electron transport was detected in dielectric BK7 glass, which was driven by high intensity contrast >1018 Wt/cm2 of fs and picoseconds (ps) laser pulses, followed by plasma expansion in vacuum for tens picoseconds [18]. This literature analysis has shown that just a few paper deal with ps-laser micromachining of glasses, although ps pulses have much higher energies (~mJ) than fs ones (~µJ) [3, 11, 13, 16, 18]. The available papers do not mention about relaxation of the drilled or micro-patterned glass, which is quite possible due to the well known viscosity of glass. New phases and new materials are formed in strongly non-equilibrium conditions of confined micro explosion. Glass mate- rials can exist in two different states (amorphous and polycrystalline) and transit between them. Besides microscopic control, any laser drilling glass technol- ogy should be monitored with structure sensitive analyses. The aim of this research was to study mechan- isms of drilling micro- and nano-size holes in optical- transparent alkali silica glass with picosecond UV- laser and (for the first time) the effects of γ- irradiation induced coloration of the glass before drilling and the following time relaxation of the drilled holes. II. Experiment Pure quartz glass (KI, KU) does not absorb UV harmonics (266 nm) of short-pulse solid Nd:YAG lasers. Therefore optical alkali silicate K8 glass was chosen, which contains the network of 51% O and 29% Si, and 11% of Na modifier (and 1% of Mg, Ca, Al) responsible for strong UV absorption band below 270 nm. Optically polished slabs had 1 mm thickness and roughness <1 µm comparable with a laser wave- length. The UV laser source was diode pumped Nd:YAG laser (PL2231-50, Ekspla, Litvania) operat- ing at the 4-th harmonic (line at 266 nm) within 20 ps pulse energy range 0.1−9 mJ (5 pulses in each train) at the repetition rate 50 Hz. Achromatic quartz lens with the focal length 75 mm was used for focusing UV laser beam to 4 µm diameter on the glass target surface. This focusing provides an energy density >108 J/m2 enough for evaporating enthalpy and ignit- ing dense plasma, so as to drill cylindrical holes in the percussive pulse mode [3, 11]. This complicated process includes a sequence of interconnected stages: non-linear optical absorption of a few photons of la- ser beam in the focal spot, avalanche ionization and energy transfer from electrons to ions, conversion of solid material into plasma, melting and evaporation by the extra heat conduction, hydrodynamic dense plasma expansion, generation of strong shockwave propagating into the surrounding cold material [13]. Surface morphology of the obtained micro- and nano- structures was investigated with scanning electron microscope (SEM-EVO-MA10, Zeiss, Germany) and X-ray diffraction with phase analysis (Empyrean, PAnalytical). The motion of a flux of charged particles inside dielectrics is limited by space charge. But due to the ionization by the strong space charge field itself (breakdown field ~5×1010 V/m) and the collision- ionization processes, a lateral expansion of the plas- ma was finally registered on-line with fast camera at the distance ~200 µm from the laser-glass interface [18]. However the scanning electron microscopy shows only “frozen” post-effect of shock waves propagating in the hot soft glass during ~30 ps of plasma existence. Fig. 4d shows the minimal conical nano-hole of elliptic shape (2 and 0.5 µm) and many nano-crystals around it at 10 times higher magnification. Since the theoretical minimum for the focal area of the 4-th harmonic is ~3×3 µm, this nanosized hole could be produced by vacuum UV-harmonics (50-100 nm) focused in submicron area 0.5×0.5 µm [12]. Domi- nating mechanisms of ionization here are multiphoton transitions through the band gap followed by heating of free electrons, ending up, in some beneficial cases, in their impact multiplication at high frequency of electron collisions with atoms [13, 18, 26]. One should emphasis, that detecting β- and γ-radiation in our case can not be attributed just to ionization, since it is caused by nuclear excitation and photonuclear reaction. Figure 5 show front (a) and back (b) views of the hole drilled at 0.9 mJ in non-irradiated glass. Entry size is ~30 µm and exit one is 13 µm. An average size of droplets is 2−3 µm.

They are the same holes in the same sample studied again and again in a month and in 2 months later (we showed only the first and last scan). This evidences about laser induced nano-crystallization of non- irradiated glass into the SiO2 crystal phase with hex- agonal symmetry. Figure 6 shows the relaxation of holes drilled at the same conditions 0.9 mJ but in the glass γ- irradiated to the dose 4×104 Gy, measured in 2 days after drilling (a) and in 2 months (b). Indeed, alkali silica glass has a high viscosity (like honey), and the deformations, caused by intensive gamma-irradiation and laser drilling, relax slowly so as to release the induced stress. Here the hole size is a little smaller than in non- irradiated glass and the droplets are of submicron size. The higher temperature of ablation, the smaller drop- lets are, as it is stated in [13]. Besides, in the left up- per corner (a) a submicron hole appears similar to Fig. 4d after drilling at >2 mJ. It can be explained by more effective absorption of the laser beam at 266 nm by γ-irradiated glass with the defect color centers and also by better focusing due to increasing of the refrac- tion index near the radiation induced color centers. Some of these centers may be related to metal collo- ids emitting surface plasmons, which interact to the laser beam [9]. Figure 6.
