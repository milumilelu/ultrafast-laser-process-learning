A two-step approach with an axial feed method for nickel-based superalloy IN792 femtosecond laser drilling was studied. The effects of different processing path parameters on the hole quality are described and discussed in detail. The drilling strategy containing a drilling through phase and an enlargement phase, which was able to produce cylindrical holes with diameters of 600 μm on 680 μm and 1300 μm thick plate without modification and reconstruction of the processing platform. According to the different material thick­ ness, the corresponding drilling optimization strategy was proposed. The experiments showed that the hole quality processed using the two-step method improved obviously compared with the one-step method, including that the outlet circularity can be increased by 7.4% and the taper angle can be reduced by 4◦. Moreover, there is no obvious heat-affected zone (HAZ), recast layer or micro-cracks on the hole surface and wall. In conclusion, the two-step approach is an effective hole processing method that provides the possibility of high-efficiency and high-quality machining of film cooling holes. 1. Introduction IN792 alloy is widely used in industrial turbine and aircraft engine components because of its excellent mechanical properties, good creep strength, and high-temperature performance [1–3]. The front inlet temperature of turbines can reach several thousand degree, but the material properties of turbine cannot deal with this high-temperature issue alone. Currently, turbine film cooling technology has become an important way to solve this problem. Therefore, the accuracy of man­ ufactured turbine film cooling holes will directly affect the performance of the engine. To achieve the cooling effect, there are thousands of small holes at 15–90◦in the curved blade surfaces, and the hole diameters are generally 0.1–1.25 mm [4,5]. With the increasing processing re­ quirements for hole drilling, traditional methods have difficulty in overcoming technological bottlenecks [6–8]. Laser drilling technology is primarily used when conventional manufacturing technologies, such as mechanical drilling or electrical discharge machining (EDM), no longer ensure successful machining [9–11]. Laser drilling relies on its extremely high power density, causing the localized material melting and vaporizing [12]. Millisecond pulse laser drilling is used extensively in high-value manufacturing to produce holes with sizes ranging from 0.25 to 1 mm, and this is a well-established technology for manufacturing various components in aircraft engine [13]. However, for all long pulse high-power laser drilling, recast layer and heat-affected zone (HAZ) are inevitable because the material removal mechanism is dominated by melt injection. Because of the rapid cooling rate of the recast layer, micro-cracks are easily generated, and may extend into the matrix, thus affecting the engine performance and shortening the service cycle [14,15]. Compared with long pulse laser processing, femtosecond laser processing has an ultrashort pulse and ultrahigh power density, and the binding force inside the matrix is not enough to curb the rapid expansion of the high-density ion electron gas so that the material is finally removed in the form of plasma eruption. Hence, femtosecond laser processing has a unique advantage in hole drilling, especially for high-quality and high-aspect-ratio holes [19,20]. Currently, many attempts have been made to identify the key process parameters affecting the various hole quality characteristics, such as hole circularity, hole taper, micro-hardness, and surface roughness, and to analyse the laser pulse interaction with the material. Huang et al. used a femtosecond fibre laser (750 fs and 1030 nm) to drill holes in both transparent and nontransparent materials (glasses, metals and tissues), and debris-free holes with good roundness and having no thermal damage were demonstrated for both glass and metal [21]. Gruner et al. studied hole drilling on stainless steel using a high repetition frequency femtosecond laser (220 fs and 1030 nm) and identified the optimum parameters for the highest available drilling quality and speed [22]. Zhang et al. advocated precision drilling with an ultrashort pulse laser (e.g., picosecond and femtosecond) to improve the quality of holes, and shown that picosecond laser helical drilling can manufacture high- quality holes with a significantly reduced recast layer thickness (<5 μm) [23]. Based on femtosecond laser processing technology and PZT (lead zirconate titanate piezoelectric ceramics) driving scanning tech­ nology, Yang et al. performed hole machining on plane or curved sur­ faces by adjusting the laser processing parameters and verified the feasibility of diesel injector nozzle processing in practical applications [7]. For hard processing materials, in addition to adjusting the parame­ ters of the laser, the adjustment and optimization of the processing technology have also achieved certain results. Jia et al. studied a com­ bined pulse laser (CPL) that consisting of an initial nanosecond pulse train followed by a millisecond pulse train for drilling the alumina ceramic, and found that the drilling method could obtain better drilling quality and solve the problem of changing absorptivity [24]. Wang et al. proposed a two-step approach using a millisecond and a femtosecond laser, and finally obtained high-quality holes on thermal barrier-coated nickel-based alloys [25]. Romoli et al. designed a micro-drilling cycle using ultrashort laser pulses based three phases in sequence: drilling through, enlargement and finishing, and these authors showed that this drilling strategy was able to produce cylindrical holes with diameters of 180 ± 2 μm on a 350 μm thick plate with the total absence of burrs and debris [26]. In view of this, after exploring the influence characteristics of the processing parameters on the drilling quality, this study upgraded and innovated the laser processing strategy, thereby achieving high- quality processing of high-aspect-ratio hole. In this work, a trepan drilling with femtosecond laser through the galvo scanning system is investigated. The novel drilling strategy con­ tains a drilling phase and an enlargement phase, i.e., the through-hole is fabricated during the first phase, and then the hole morphology is optimized by the second phase. The two-step drilling method is completed on the same platform without upgrading and rebuilding the processing equipment. There is no need to change the laser or the machining platform, that is, the hole was repaired in situ without the error of secondary machining. Although the processing speed of galvo scanning system is fast, the machining taper of hole is relatively large. The addition of enlargement phase not only reduces the hole taper but also modifies the roundness of outlet in time. In addition, the surface and cross-sectional morphology of the holes were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and laser scanning confocal microscopy (LSCM) with regard to geometrical and metallurgical characteristics. Furthermore, through the innovation and optimization of the technol­ ogy, the high-efficiency and high-quality machining of single hole and hole array can be realized. Most of all, this drilling method is simple, convenient and feasible, which provides technical reference and theo­ retical support for high-aspect-ratio hole drilling and difficult machined material processing. 2. Experiment procedure 2.1. Materials, equipment, and experimental design The aerospace nickel-based superalloy IN792 is selected as the workpiece material. A size of 20 mm × 20 mm × 0.68 mm was used for the samples (unless otherwise stated). The chemical composition of IN792 is listed in Table 1. Prior to laser drilling, all samples were cleaned for approximately 10 min with an ultrasonic cleaning machine (KQ- 50DE, Kunshan ultrasonic instruments co., LTD.) to avoid the impact of fragments and contaminants from the sample fabrication process. The micro-machining system includes a femtosecond laser system and a five-axis motion platform. A femtosecond laser (Monaco, Coherent) with a temporal width of approximately 400 fs-10 ps, an maximum average output power of 40 W, a wavelength of 1035 nm and repetition rate of 1 MHz was used. As shown in Fig. 1, the optical design was composed of a beam expander, a galvo scanning system (Scanlab IntelliSCAN14), and several mirrors. All experiments mentioned in this article were performed under the same conditions of clean air (Class 1000 clean room) and constant temperature (25 ◦C). The machining system was also equipped with CCD camera system that is used for target alignment, focus confirmation, as well as preliminary imaging/evalua­ tion of the samples surfaces before and after processing. Holes with di­ ameters of 600 μm were selected to be processed in the experiments. The laser beam was directly focused on the IN792 surface using field lens with a focal length of 100 mm resulting in a focal spot diameter of approximately 16 μm, and the depth of focus is about 0.1 mm. The laser drilling process was performed in ambient air without shielding gas, as Fig. 1 shown. After laser drilling, the samples were washed with alcohol and dried for analysis by SEM (S-3400 N, Hitachi) and LSCM (VK-X1000, Keyence). Effect of scanning path on the laser drilling quality The machining quality in laser drilling is highly influenced by pro­ cessing path parameters, so it is essential to explore the influence of each parameter on the machining process and determine the appropriate parameters. Since the laser scanning path is controlled by the galvo scanning system, the selection and design of the scanning path have a great influence on the drilling quality. The scanning path for hole machining generally includes linear scanning, spiral scanning, nested circular scanning (inside-out and outside-in), as shown in Fig. 2 (a-d). For the linear scanning path (Fig. 3 (a)), the start and end points of each laser scanning route were located at the edge of the hole, because of the discontinuity of the scanning path, the edge of the inlet was irregular. For spiral scanning (Fig. 3 (b)), because the end of the path was not closed, there was a defect similar to a small tail at the inlet edge. For the nested circular scanning path (Fig. 3 (c) and (d)), the two scanning processes with opposite paths lead to completely different drilling results. In the early stage of drilling, the hole taper is larger, that is, the inlet diameter is smaller than the outlet. As shown in Fig. 2 (e), during nested circular scanning from inside to outside, the laser was propagated in the air without any hindrance, and its energy was basically lossless. In addition, the laser ablated the material from the inside to the outside, resulting in that the ablated debris can be splashed out both from the inlet and outlet. Therefore, the material was gradually removed, and the hole shape was gradually optimized. However, compared to Fig. 2 (e), Fig. 2 (f) shown the sanning schematic diagram of nested circular scanning from outside to inside. During the laser axial feed process, since the scanning starts at the edge part of the hole instead of the center part, the material was not completely removed, which caused the laser propagation to be blocked, and the energy cannot reach the bottom of the material, resulting in a low material removal rate. Moreover, the ablated debris can only be discharged from the inlet. With the increase of ablation products, if the debris is not discharged in time, it will accumulate on the hole wall, causing repeated remelting of the ablation product, thereby affecting the hole processing quality, especially the outlet morphology [17]. There­ fore, the scanning path from inside to outside can be machined a better hole than the scanning path from outside to inside. Effect oflaser pulse energy on the laser drilling quality An experiment that verified the influence of pulse energy on hole drilling was carried out. The scanning speed was set to 200 mm/s, the feeding distance was set to 60 μm, the feeding times was set to 8, and the scanning times for each layer was set to 80. Then, the pulse energies of 120 μJ, 100 μJ, 80 μJ, 60 μJ and 40 μJ were selected. Under the selected processing parameters, the through hole can be formed by feeding down 1 to 4 times. Fig. 5 shows the hole morphology observed by SEM under different pulse energies. In addition, the dimension precision data of the holes were calculated and shown in Table 3.It can be found that when the pulse energy was 120 μJ, although the inlet circularity was better, the outlet circularity was poor (80.3%) As the pulse energy decreased from 100 μJ to 40 μJ, the circularity of the inlet and outlet of the hole decreased gradually (92.2% to 84.4%), and the taper increased from 4.6◦to 9.6◦. When the pulse energy was 100 μJ, the processing effect achieved best, the circularity of the inlet and outlet can reached 99.39% and 92.26%, respectively, and the taper angle was reduced to 4.6–6.2◦. That is, the high pulse energy will lead to the remelting of the spatter, while the low energy will make the material unable to be completely ablated. In both cases, the material can not be discharged from the orifice in time, leading to the poor circularity of the outlet. 3.3.
