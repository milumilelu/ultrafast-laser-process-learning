[Methods] First, a discrete Fresnel diffraction propagation model was established for two-dimensional gratings to explore the self-imaging characteristics of different types of two-dimensional gratings, and grating parameters were designed based on the self-imaging position and period. Then, four combinations of Checkerboard and Mesh gratings with π and π/2 phase shifts were simulated using Python to analyze their Talbot self-imaging characteristics. The thickness required for π phase shifts and photon transmission rates for various materials were analyzed at 8 keV and 1 keV photon energies. Finally, the imaging process based on a polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) sphere was simulated using the designed detector parameters, and the wavefront phase was extracted using Fourier fringe analysis to verify the feasibility of the method. [Results] Two-dimensional wavefront detector parameters suitable for both hard X-ray (8 keV) and soft X-ray (1 keV) bands are successfully designed, with phase grating thickness of 10.1 μm and 1.38 μm for silicon material, respectively. The Checkerboard-π phase grating combined with a Mesh absorption grating is determined to be the optimal configuration. The extracted PMMA sphere wavefront phase distribution shows excellent consistency in both perpendicular directions, with integrated phase shifts proportional to the sphere thickness. [Conclusions] This study provides a framework and theoretical reference for the development of X-ray two-dimensional wavefront detectors. Compared with traditional one-dimensional grating methods, the proposed detector can obtain wavefront phase information in two perpendicular directions simultaneously, offering greater flexibility and adaptability across both soft and hard X-ray energy ranges. The design parameters and phase recovery method demonstrate high accuracy and feasibility for advanced X-ray wavefront characterization systems.
