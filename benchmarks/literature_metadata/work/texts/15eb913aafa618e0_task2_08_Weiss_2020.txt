A R T I C L E I N F O Keywords: Ultrafast laser Microhole drilling Process parameter Geometric precision Surface quality A B S T R A C T The geometric precision and surface quality of ﬁlm cooling holes has signiﬁcant inﬂuence on the cooling eﬃ- ciency and fatigue life of turbine blades. In this paper, the investigation about fabrication process of ﬁlm cooling holes on the single crystal superalloy DD6 plate specimen by ultrafast laser is carried out. By comparing three diﬀerent processing paths, minimum roughness of hole wall could be obtained by concentric circular scanning, which was used in the followed-up experiment. The inﬂuence of process parameters on the geometric precision and surface quality of microholes was analyzed, and the physical mechanism was examined. The results show that among several process parameters, focus position has the most signiﬁcant inﬂuence on the microholes geometric precision and surface quality, and that the divergent beam (where the focal plane is above the ma- chined surface) can lead to smaller taper, roundness, and surface roughness. In addition, besides a layer of solidiﬁed debris adheres to the inner wall at the hole entrance, the microholes machined by ultrafast lasers have no other defects such as a recast layer, microcrack or heat aﬀected zone. 1. Introduction At present, the ﬁlm cooling and hollow turbine blade technology have been used to reduce the surface temperature of turbine blade, which could improve the operation eﬃciency and service life of turbine engine [1]. The turbine blade with ﬁlm cooling holes is shown in Fig. 1 [2]. Compared with electric discharge machining (EDM), electro- chemical machining (ECM) and traditional nanosecond laser ma- chining, ultrafast laser machining with pulse duration below 10 ps have the potential to become a promising processing method for manu- facturing ﬁlm cooling holes due to the advantages of small heat aﬀected zone, good surface quality and processing ultra-hard and brittle mate- rials [3]. However, ultrafast laser machining can cause geometric de- fects such as taper and roundness error [4] and surface defects such as thin recast and oxide layers [5]. These defects could damage structure integrity and cause local stress concentration, resulting in fatigue failure in high temperature and pressure environments. Therefore, it is of great importance to study ultrafast laser processing and its physical mechanism to improve geometric precision and surface quality of ﬁlm cooling holes. Many studies have been carried out on the theory and experiment of ultrafast laser processing since the 1970s. Anisimov et al. proposed a two-temperature model, which utilized a diﬀerence equation described electrons and crystal lattice temperature variations to explain the laser- metal interaction mechanism [6]. Based on this research, eﬀorts have been made to further study the metal laser ablation process. Liang et al. established a theoretical model to predict the ablation threshold of stainless steel based on the width of ablation channels, and veriﬁed their model with ablation experiments [8]. Feng et al. conducted ablation experiments on single crystal alloys and thermal barrier coated by femtosecond lasers. The ablation thresholds of single crystal alloys and thermal barrier coating were 0.33 J/cm2 and 1.52 J/cm2, respectively [9,10]. Semaltianos et al. measured the single- pulse ablation threshold was 0.26 J/cm2 with a femtosecond laser ab- lation nickel-based alloy C263 [11]. Ma et al. carried out femtosecond laser ablation experiments on single crystal superalloy CMSX-4 and the individual components Ni and Ni3Al. The results showed that with the increase of laser ﬂuence, two distinct ablation morphology were ap- peared and their ablation thresholds were 0.3 and 5.3 J/cm2, respec- tively [12]. Wang et al. investigated the inﬂuence of focus position on ablation microgroove shape, and found convergent beam with the focus plane below the machined surface is tend to fabricate V-shape micro- groove, while divergent beam is tend to fabricate U-shape microgroove [13]. All these ablation theories and experiments of metals with ul- trafast laser have provided strong support for the fabrication of ﬁlm cooling holes. Recently, scholars have studied ultrafast laser drilling of alloys. Das and Pollock studied the ﬁlm cooling holes machining by femtosecond laser on superalloy CMSX4 with thermal barrier coatings (TBCs), it was found that the ﬁlm cooling holes have no surface defects such as recast layers and microcracks; however, there was a certain taper due to the Gaussian energy distribution of pulse lasers [14]. Wang et al. examined the inﬂuence of process parameters on the quality of microholes and it was noted that the spiral trepanning method achieved best quality [15]. Yu et al. used picosecond lasers to drill the titanium alloy TC4 and investigated the inﬂuence of laser ﬂuence and pulse numbers on the material removal rate. It was found that the higher laser ﬂuence and lower pulse number were conducive to increasing the material removal rate [16]. Sun et al. found that a thin intermittent resolidiﬁed layer existed in the hole wall by trepan drilling of Inconel 718 with TBCs with picosecond lasers, and that this layer was completely diﬀerent from the recast layer by long-pulse laser drilling [17]. The geometric precision and surface quality of ﬁlm cooling holes has a great inﬂuence on turbine blade performance. From the analysis above, the geometric precision and surface quality of ﬁlm cooling hole by ultrafast laser has not been widely and deeply investigated. To ad- dress this gap, this study investigated the inﬂuence of process para- meters and paths on the geometric precision and surface quality of microholes on plate specimen of nickel-based single crystal alloy with ultrafast lasers. Three diﬀerent scanning paths in the laser drilling process were selected for comparison. The eﬀects of machining parameters (mean power, scanning velocity, focus position, scanning width and blowing pressure) on microhole roundness, taper, surface roughness and surface topography are also analyzed to better under- stand the laser drilling process. 2. Materials and experimental methods 2.1. Materials and experiment setup The material used in this study was the second-generation nickel- based superalloy DD6 provided by Beijing Institute of Aeronautical Materials, China. The elementary composition of DD6 is shown in Table 1. The schematic diagram of the ultrafast laser processing system adopted in this work is shown in Fig. 2. The commercially available diode-pumped solid lasers (Altantic series, Lithuania) was used in this work, which emits pulse laser with a wavelength of 1030 nm and a pulse width of 2.1 ps pulse laser. Other parameters of laser processing system are listed in Table 2. The beam is focused by a focus lens with a focal length of 150 mm. The laser process are assisted by coaxial gas blowing and observed in real time with a CCD camera. The scanning speed is adjusted by the scanning galvanometer, while 3-D mobile platform is used to adjust the relative position of laser beam and spe- cimen. 2.2. Experimental design and measurement device In this work, the inﬂuence of process parameters and paths on the geometric precision and surface quality of microhole was investigated. It can be seen from Fig. 5(a) and (b) that a smooth microhole surface is achieved by the concentric cir- cular scanning path, and that the linear scanning path produces micro holes with obvious periodic surface microwave in the hole wall. Com- pared with linear scanning, the hole wall with linear + circular scan- ning is relatively smooth but still exist periodic surface microwave, as presented in Fig. 5(c). To further analyze the surface quality of the microholes with three scanning paths, the hole-wall roughness was measured, as shown in Fig. 6. It can be seen that the hole-wall roughness of the concentric circular scanning path is the smallest while the roughness of the linear scanning path is the largest due to the obvious stair-step eﬀect on the hole wall, as shown in Fig. 5(b). In comparison, the linear + circular scanning method slightly decreases stair-step eﬀect as displayed in Fig. 5(c), but still has a larger surface roughness. Based on this analysis, the concentric circular scanning method was chosen for all the drilling experiments. 3.2. Scanning velocity The inﬂuence of scanning velocity on geometric precision of mi- croholes as shown in Fig. 9. It can be seen that this eﬀect is negligible. During laser processing, scanning velocity directly aﬀect the spot overlap rate of pulse laser. So a lower scanning velocity can increases pulse overlap rate and cause energy accumulation, which could further increase laser ﬂuence of ablation region and decrease the material ab- lation threshold due to incubation eﬀect [12]. Section 3.2.1 shows that the increase of laser ﬂuence can increase material removal rate, re- sulting in an increase in the diameters of the entrance and exit holes. Laser scanning strategy applied in the experiments is shown in Fig. 10. Based on Eq. (5), the laser overlap rates corresponding to the scanning velocities are 99.7, 98.3 and 97.2%, respectively. Therefore, it can be concluded that among the selected parameters, the pulse overlap rate is high and their diﬀerence between the three scanning velocities is not obvious. Ac- cording to Li et al. [7], when the pulse number exceed a certain value, the incubation eﬀect becomes saturated and the material ablation threshold keeps nearly unchanged. Besides, Zhao et al. found the ma- terial ablation rate decrease signiﬁcant with the increase of pulse number [26]. Therefore, it can be inferred that when the pulse overlap rate exceed a certain value, the increase of the pulse overlap rate has little eﬀect on the material removal rate due to the saturation eﬀect. So the scanning velocity selected in this work has a negligible eﬀect on the geometric precision of microholes. 3.2.3. Focus position Fig. 11 presents the inﬂuence of focus position on microholes geo- metric precision. It can be seen that focus position has a signiﬁcant inﬂuence on geometric precision. With a positive defocus distance (the focus plane is above the machined surface), a small entrance diameter, large exit diameter, and small exit roundness and taper can be obtained, while the taper of the microholes with a negative defocus distance is about twice that with a positive defocus distance.
