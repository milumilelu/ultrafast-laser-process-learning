[PAGE 1] Vol.:(0123456789) 1 3 Archives of Civil and Mechanical Engineering (2022) 22:141 https://doi.org/10.1007/s43452-022-00465-x REVIEW ARTICLE Insights into drilling film cooling holes on ceramic‑coated nickel‑based superalloys Balaji Vasudevan1 · Yuvaraj Natarajan1 · Vinothkumar Sivalingam2 · Grzegorz Krolczyk3 · Puneet Tandon4 Received: 26 January 2022 / Revised: 20 April 2022 / Accepted: 16 May 2022 / Published online: 8 June 2022 © Wroclaw University of Science and Technology 2022 Abstract This article aims at providing insights into various drilling operations relating to the production of holes of small diam- eter for film cooling, particularly inclined holes, in aero-engine turbine blades, made of Yttria-stabilized Zirconia (YSZ) coated superalloy. Drilling inclined holes on YSZ coated superalloys that find use in aerospace industries is difficult due to the presence of two sections of materials, each section possessing different material properties. This article indicates five machining processes, namely conventional drilling, electrical discharge drilling, laser drilling, electro chemical discharge machining (ECDM), and abrasive water jet machining (AWJM). Machinability and the challenges involved in the fabrication of micro straight and inclined holes on ceramic-coated nickel-based superalloys have been discussed. Previous researchers have attempted at drilling tiny holes (≤ 2 mm) on ceramic-coated and uncoated nickel-based superalloys at different hole angles varying from 10° to 90°. Material removal was seen as difficult to control in the YSZ coated section when compared to the superalloys. Despite the occurrence of a controlled manner of material removal in different machining techniques, the holes produced had poor geometry and surface finish with cavities on the ceramic coat and burr formation at the hole exit. There was also the problem of thermal spalling, delamination, flakes, spatters, recast layer and cracks seen over the ceramic coat due to the employment of thermal-based machining techniques including laser drilling with advanced pulse settings. Keeping this in view, the AWJM technique is found to be a good alternative technique for drilling inclined holes with size constraints as the cold process maintains material integrity, followed by the ECDM and the conventional drilling processes. However, the hybrid process, namely ECDM, has still been a complex phenomenon in the material removal mechanism of the YSZ coated section consuming a lot of time. Based on the key results from the different setups, the outcome of this work provides a platform for scientists and engineers working in aerospace industries for finding a suitable machining process for drilling precision holes on the YSZ coated nickel-based superalloys. [PAGE 2] Archives of Civil and Mechanical Engineering (2022) 22:141 1 3 141 Page 2 of 30 1 Introduction Gas turbines find extensive use in jet engines, power gen- eration and several industrial applications. The gas turbine is a type of external combustion engine, in which the gas is produced through the burning of the air fuel mixture in the combustion chamber. The gas with high pressure and high temperature of around 1400 °C spins the turbine to generate power. The thermal efficiency of the gas turbine is improved through an increase in the combustion tem- perature but, on the other hand, it causes deterioration in material properties and increases fuel consumption [1]. Therefore, the focus is required on the development of a new cooling method or suitable material for resisting high temperatures. Ceramic coated nickel-based superal- loys are mostly preferred for hot jet engine sections due to their high-temperature corrosion resistance, oxidation resistance, creep resistance and strength [2]. Figure 1 shows the ceramic-coated superalloy, thermal barrier coating (TBC) reducing the quantum of heat trans- ferred to the metal substrate from the combustion tem- perature. Yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) was commonly employed for TBC in the hot section components due to its high thermal stability, strength, hardness and porous microstructure [1]. TBC has a two-layered structure in which the ceramic top layer is the actual thermal barrier reducing heat transfer to the substrate. The bond coat is a mixture of different materials that include Ni, Al, Co, Cr, M, Ta and Y. The bond coat ensures interface bond- ing between ceramic coating and substrate in addition to providing oxidation resistance. In addition, a Thermally Grown Oxide (TGO) layer grows at the interface between the ceramic coat and bond coat at high temperatures. Despite the metal substrate coated with a ceramic layer on top and developing the TGO, the jet engine turbine blade is not suitable to operate or withstand at high-temperature gas stream due to the degradation of material properties. In an advanced gas turbine engine, the combustion tem- perature reaches around 1600–1800 °C, which is above the permissible temperature level of nickel superalloy used in turbine blades. In view of this, researchers have proposed cooling holes on turbine blades for improvement in the efficiency of the jet engine. Figure 2 shows the gas turbine blade with cooling holes. Film cooling involves cooling of the surface layer of air, which reduces the heat flow of hot gas to the deeper section of the turbine blade. Reduction in heat flow helps reduction in the thermal stress load on the blade, the economy in fuel consumption and enhancement of jet speed and blade life span [3]. Figure 3 shows the external cooling of the gas turbine blade through film cooling holes. The size of the cooling holes ranges from 300 to 3000 µm. Machining an array of inclined micro holes with various profile geometries on turbine blade continues to be a challenging job for industries until now. Variations were made in the hole angles were varied from 0°/90° to 35° for film cooling. However, the 25° holes were found to have a higher blowing ratio resulting in effective film cooling performance [4]. Compared to cylindrical holes, inclined holes and barrel-shaped holes offer much higher film cooling efficacy in the turbine blades [3]. Machining accuracy plays a major role in bringing down the tempera- ture and improving the cooling efficiency of the turbine blade. This can be further improved by the fabrication of precise inclined cooling holes on the turbine blade [5]. The drilling process plays a vital role in the fabrication of holes; it produces precise holes with a smooth surface finish. However, the machining of YSZ coated superal- loy is quite difficult due to its high strength and hardness. In addition, its sandwich structure (nonmetal and metal combination) decreases the machinability as the YSZ and nickel-based superalloy contains different mechanical and thermal properties. This may often lead to tool failure as the tool wears out during the conventional drilling of sev- eral holes. Also, low thermal conductivity and high abra- sion resistance of the YSZ ceramic section cause hindrance to material removal by different machining techniques. However, researchers have adopted improved conventional and unconventional machining techniques considering the higher abrasive resistance and the melting point. Currently, many researchers have attempted the machin- ing of cooling holes in YSZ coated nickel-based superal- loys using different machining processes. But industries are looking for a suitable technique for the production of precise cooling holes. Some researchers have provided critical reviews on drilling straight holes rather than inclined holes on ceramic coated materials. But there is no report related to the current issue of drilling inclined holes through the use of advanced machining techniques, such as Electro Chemical Machining (ECM) and Abrasive Water Jet (AWJ) machining.
