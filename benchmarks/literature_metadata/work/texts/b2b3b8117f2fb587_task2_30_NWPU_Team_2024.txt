In this paper, a segmented processing method of fan-shaped FCHs is proposed, which realizes high- quality processing of fan-shaped FCHs. The effects of processing parameters on the machining efficiency, geo­ metric accuracy and surface morphology of fan-shaped FCHs were analyzed. And the microstructure, composi­ tion, surface roughness and residual stress of the hole wall under specific processing parameters were characterized. The influence mechanism of processing parameters on the processed results is clarified by laser overlap rate and laser energy density of the processing area. The important role of focus compensation in the segmented machining method is discussed. The formation mechanism of microstructures was explained by the plasma exciton theory and the energy shielding effect. Furthermore, the process method is promoted through expansion section model segmentation and processing attitude adjustment to realize the processing of cat-ear- shaped and bat-face-shaped FCHs. 1. Introduction Turbine is the component with the largest thermal load and me­ chanical load in aero-engine, and the service environment of turbine blades is particularly harsh. During the operation of the engine, the turbine blades need to withstand high temperature, high pressure and complex stress, so its manufacturing technology is listed as one of the key technologies of modern aero-engine [1,2]. With the continuous improvement of the inlet temperature of the turbine, it is not enough to meet this temperature requirement only by relying on the temperature resistance of the turbine blade material itself. Therefore, the film cooling technology is developed to improve the operating temperature of the turbine blade [3,4]. The principle of film cooling technology is to pro­ cess multiple rows of film cooling holes (FCHs) on the turbine blades, so that the cooling airflow is ejected from the FCHs to form a cooling film on the blade surface, blocking the high-temperature gas, thereby avoiding the direct impact of high-temperature gas on the blades (Fig. 1 (a) (b)) [5,6]. The film cooling technology greatly improves the per­ formance of the engine and is one of the most representative advanced technologies in aero-engine technology. The most common form of the FCHs is a circular hole. However, many studies have shown that the cooling film in the downstream region of the circular FCHs is extremely uneven and fails to form effective coverage. This leads to an increase in the temperature of the blade surface, resulting in significant thermal stress that may cause blade failure [7,8]. In recent years, advancements in FCH geometry have been driven by the pursuit of higher cooling ef­ ficiency. Goldstein and Burggraf [9] pioneered the development of FCHs with spanwise expansion angles at the outlet, which effectively reduce downstream film cooling inhomogeneity (Fig. 1 (c)). This innovative design improves turbine blade cooling efficiency by optimizing coolant distribution [10–12]. Rational design of shaped FCHs not only enhances cooling performance but also reduces the total number of FCHs required. Fewer FCHs result in lower cooling system demands and shorter manufacturing cycles, leading to improved engine power output and reduced turbine blade production costs. Building upon conventional fan- shaped configurations, researchers have developed advanced FCH ge­ ometries including cat-ear, dumbbell, crescent, and other complex- profile variants (Fig. 1 (d)-(g)) [13–15]. Conventional mechanical machining struggles to fabricate shaped FCHs, while existing feasible methods inevitably introduce dimensional deviations during processing. These deviations result in geometric discrepancies between as - fabri­ cated and designed FCHs. Additionally, suboptimal processes often generate surface quality defects, including high roughness, recast layers, heat - affected zones, and microcracks. When the turbine blades serve in harsh environments, the geometric inaccuracies and surface flaws of the FCHs not only reduce the film cooling efficiency, but also pose a huge threat to the structural strength of the turbine blades, thus affecting the service life and operational reliability of the engine [16–22]. Therefore, developing ideal manufacturing methods for shaped FCHs is critical to advancing aero - engine technology. In the past, research on shaped FCHs has primarily focused on hole geometry design and corresponding cooling performance [12–15,23,24]. However, studies on their manufacturing processes remain severely limited. Existing literature indicates that electrical discharge machining (EDM) serves as the dominant fabrication method for shaped FCHs. Additionally, emerging techniques such as additive manufacturing (AM), direct casting, hybrid machining, and laser manufacturing (LP) have been explored in recent years. For EDM, Kliuev et al. [25,26] demonstrated the feasibility of drilling diffusion holes on turbine blades using EDM. They innovatively integrated circular hole drilling with diffusion hole formation, employing a single electrode for sequential operations (Fig. 2 (a)). Liang et al. [27] developed a segmented EDM approach for diffusion hole fabrication, introducing self-repairing strategies for tool electrode wear and contour error compensation (Fig. 2 (b)). Rouina et al. [28] experimentally investigated geometric factors (hole diameter, aspect ratio, injection angle and transverse expansion angle) affecting cooling performance in EDM - processed shaped FCHs. Their results confirmed that V-shaped holes can be reliably manufactured with proper expansion angle tolerancing (Fig. 2 (c)). Wang et al. [29] proposed a multi-axis fast EDM milling method for turbine blade diffusion holes, enabling fabrication of fan- shaped and conical FCHs while implementing electrode wear compen­ sation strategies (Fig. 2 (d)). Regarding AM, Stimpson et al. [30] utilized metal powder AM technology to produce fan-shaped FCHs with two different diameters. However, cooling efficiency tests revealed that this technology struggles to reliably generate shaped FCHs with predictable performance (Fig. 2 (e)). Furgeson et al. [31] constructed shaped FCHs via direct laser metal sintering AM technology, quantifying geometric deviations using computed tomography (CT). Their results highlighted oversized holes and excessive surface roughness (Fig. 2 (f)). For direct casting, Wu et al. [32] combined stereolithography and gel casting to prototype turbine blade FCHs. However, it is clear that the hole size they cast is much larger than the common size of the FCHs, and it is difficult to mass-produce (Fig. 2 (g)). For combined machining, Okasha et al. [33] demonstrated sequential laser-mechanical drilling for acute-angle microholes in In718 alloy. This hybrid method produced expanded- section holes with reduced burrs and processing time compared to conventional drilling (Fig. 2 (h)). For LM, Siemens Energy AG [34] has developed a laser system with different pulse duration combinations for the fabrication of shaped FCHs with complex 3D geometries (Fig. 2 (i)). The Mound Laser & Photonics Center [35] fabricated simple slope-like and finger-like shaped holes (Fig. 2 (j)) on the TBC coating using pico­ second laser. He et al. [36] proposed a three-dimensional drilling strategy for machining symmetrically shaped holes using helical drilling optics and ultrashort pulse laser radiation, and fabricated a micro-funnel with an opening angle of 90◦on a 0.5 mm thick stainless steel (Fig. 2 (k)). Zhang et al. [37] combined femtosecond laser drilling with abra­ sive flow machining to produce diffusion holes, and fatigue testing showed a 50.6 % lifespan improvement over unpolished holes. The diffusion hole processed by this technology has no defects such as recast layer, micro cracks or heat affected zone, but there are significant so­ lidified debris in the hole wall (Fig. 2 (l)). In summary, although some pioneering work has been done on the processing technology of shaped FCHs, the related research is still insufficient. And most of the research is only the presentation of pro­ cessing technology, lack of in-depth discussion on the process-property relationship, the multi-dimensional characterization of processing re­ sults and the forming mechanism of processing. Furthermore, from the actual results of various processing technologies in Fig. 2, the shaped FCHs processed by various processing technologies generally have problems of geometric deviation and poor surface quality. Therefore, this study proposes a segmented processing method for fan-shaped FCHs, considering their geometric configurations and manufacturing characteristics. Through multi-dimensional character­ ization of processing outcomes under different parameters, the influence of process parameters on fan-shaped FCHs and their formation mecha­ nisms are analyzed. Furthermore, this method is extended to fabricate two additional complex shaped FCH. The results provide a novel refer­ ence method for manufacturing turbine blade FCHs and advance the application of ultrafast laser precision machining in aviation manufacturing. Fig. 1. [PAGE 4] 2. Experiment 2.1. Materials The test material is a nickel-based single crystal superalloy widely used in high-pressure turbine blades of aero-engines. The main elemental composition of the test alloy is shown in Table 1. The test alloy was cast by directional solidification process, the growth direction was controlled to be [001] crystal orientation, and the microstructure was adjusted by multi-stage heat treatment. The physical picture of the tested alloy is shown in Fig. 3 (a), and its length direction is [001] orientation. The original microstructure of the test alloy is shown in Fig. 3 (b), which is composed of γ phase and γ’ phase. Considering the orientation and wall thickness of the turbine blade, a plate with a thickness of 1.5 mm was used as the sample, and the length direction of the sample was parallel to the [001] orientation. The processed exper­ imental sample with fan-shaped FCHs is shown in Fig. 3 (c). 2.2. Parameterized geometric model of fan-shaped FCH The model and parametric modeling method of the fan-shaped FCH are shown in Fig. 4. The modeling includes the following steps: Step1: established a fan-shaped FCH formation model, and parame­ terized the geometric features of the formation model. As shown in Fig. 4 (a), the forming model includes the following geometric features: α is the main inclination angle, β is the forward dip angle, θ is the expansion angle, D is the aperture of the metering section, L2 is the length of the metering section, L1 is the mapping distance of the expansion section in the formation model, A is the half length of the mapping surface, B is the height of the mapping surface, and R is the chamfer of the mapping surface. According to the geometric formation relationship, some geometric features satisfy the following expressions: A = ( L1 + D 2tanθ ) × tanθ + R (1) B = L1 × tanβ + D (2) Step2: Perform Boolean intersection operation (Fig. 4 (b)) on the fan- shaped FCH formation model and the parts to be processed to obtain the fan-shaped FCH model including the metering section and the expansion section (Fig. 4 (c)). Since the geometric features of the fan-shaped FCH formation model are parameterized, the fan-shaped FCH with different geometric pa­ rameters can be quickly and easily generated by modifying the above geometric features. It can be seen from Fig. 4 that the established fan-shaped FCH mainly contains four geometric parameters: α, β, θ and D. In this study, geo­ metric models with α = 45◦, β = 10◦, θ = 10◦and D = 0.4 mm were selected for subsequent femtosecond laser processing experiments. 2.3. Femtosecond laser processing system with double processing heads The femtosecond laser processing system is mainly composed of femtosecond laser, laser cooling module, galvanometer scanning pro­ cessing head, helical drilling processing head, air compressor, five-axis mobile platform, the laser range finder, the positioning camera and controller. The schematic diagram of the femtosecond laser processing system with double processing heads is shown in Fig. 5 (a). The system can realize the automatic switching of the optical path through the conversion device, to realize the two processing modes of galvanometer scanning processing and helical drilling processing. The femtosecond solid-state Yb: KGW laser can generate a typical Gaussian beam with TEM00 transverse mode. And the intensity distribution of the laser beam in the propagation direction and the focal plane drawn by the visuali­ zation software is shown in Fig. 5 (b). The laser cooling module cools the running laser. The galvanometer scanning processing head can realize the three-dimensional trajectory planning during laser scanning and is used to process the expansion section of the shaped FCHs. The helical drilling processing head can perform laser scanning of the spiral tra­ jectory, which is used to process the metering section of the fan-shaped FCHs. The specimen is fixed on the five-axis mobile platform through the tooling fixture, so that the attitude adjustment of five degree of freedom can be carried out. The repeated positioning accuracy of the moving axis of the five-axis mobile platform is 6 μm, and the repeated positioning accuracy of the rotating axis is 20″. The processing position is accurately positioned through the laser range finder, the positioning camera and the five-axis mobile platform. In the process of processing, the high- pressure gas is continuously blown to the processing area through the air compressor, thereby promoting the discharge of the removed mate­ rial. Processing method of fan-shaped FCHs The general idea of processing fan-shaped FCHs in this study is to process the expansion section and the metering section respectively. Firstly, the expansion section is processed by the galvanometer scanning processing head of the femtosecond processing system, and then the metering section is processed by the helical drilling processing head.
