However, the weakness of bonding interface between sintered Ag NPs and bare Cu substrate limits the applications of the Ag NPs paste, thereby reducing the shear strength of the sintered joint. In this work, ultrafast laser processing is utilized to enhance the bonding strength of the sintered Ag joint by fabricating a microstructure interface. The microstructure dimensions are tunable by controlling laser parameters, and then high- strength joints could be obtained. Different substrate microstructures were constructed, and the enhanced bonding mechanism was analyzed by characterizing the cross section and fracture surface morphologies of joints. The ultrafast laser processing could increase the surface energy of Cu substrates to form a more reliable connection with Ag NPs and more energy required for crack extension with the increasing connection area, thereby resulting in a significant improvement in the shear strength of the Ag NP joints. The patterned microstructures on the Cu substrate using this technique showed improved surface energy and increased number of connection areas on the substrate, showing potential for the use in third-generation semiconductors for highly reliable packaging. INTRODUCTION Silicon carbide (SiC)1–4 and gallium nitride (GaN)5,6 along with other representatives of the third-generation semiconductor devices are developing rapidly since they have a high bandgap,7 high thermal conductivity,8 and excellent electrical properties,9 which can be utilized in service at a temperature of more than 300 °C.10 Traditional Sn-based solder has a low melting point and cannot meet the operating temperature of power devices.11 Silver nanoparticle (Ag NP) paste has excellent thermal and mechanical properties and is a promising electronic packaging material.12–15 Ag NPs with a high surface energy and high conductivity can be made to connect at low temperatures and withstand high operating tem- peratures to realize the concept of “low-temperature bond, high- temperature service” and provide reliable support and protection for the die.16–19 However, the bonding among Ag nanoparticles and the bare Cu substrate is time-consuming and challenging20 due to the low surface energy of planar substrates, weakening the bonding of Journal of Laser Applications ARTICLE pubs.aip.org/lia/jla J. Ultrafast laser processing has many advantages in preparing micro/nanostructures owing to their ultrashort pulse time, extremely high irradiance, and three-dimensional ultraprecision.24 Ultrafast laser processing-patterned surface structures have been widely used in material bonding,25–27 but the research for electronic packaging has not been comprehensively studied yet. In a previous study, hierarchical microscale tapered arrays prepared using a fem- tosecond laser could significantly improve the shear strength of Ag NPs.28,29 However, the mechanism of its enhanced connection needs further exploration, and the influence of different patterns has not been evident so far. In this work, the patterned microstructures on the bare Cu surface were fabricated by ultrafast laser processing, and the strength of sintered Ag NP joints was studied in detail. We investi- gated ultrafast laser processing by tuning the intensity of lasers and constructed different microstructure substrates by changing the parameters of the laser process. We conducted shear tests on sin- tered Ag NP joints of various substrates to explore the influence of other interfaces. Finally, we investigated the mechanism of enhanced joints by analyzing the impact of different surface micro- structures on the shear strength of joints. This work can potentially guide the micropatterned designs on the metallic substrate to increase the joint bonding using the ultrafast laser processing approach. II. MATERIALS AND METHODS The microstructures were constructed on the bare Cu substrate (10 × 10 × 1 mm3), where the oxide layer of Cu was sanded and pol- ished before the laser process, and the surface was subsequently cleaned using ethanol to remove the oil and other dirties. The laser process was carried out using a picosecond laser (PX100-1 InnoSlab Laser, EdgeWave) with a wavelength of 1064 nm, a pulse duration of 12 ps, laser pulse energy varied from 40 to 167 μJ, and a repetition rate of 400 kHz. A high-speed scanning galvanometer (intelliSCAN, Scanlab) and an F-theta field mirror (SL-1064-45-100Q, Wavelength OE) were used to scan and focus the laser beam to about 25 μm in diameter, and the scanning speed was set at 3000 mm/s. The precise construction of the microstructure was achieved by modifying differ- ent laser power (P), scanning times (n), and scanning distance (d). After the laser process, the substrate was ultrasonically cleaned with acid (5% H2SO4) for 5 min to remove surface oxides then washed with anhydrous ethanol for 10 min. The organics used consisted of three parts: binders, solvent, and dispersant, which have different volatil- ization temperatures to ensure high-quality sintering effects. The binder’s selection included ethyl cellulose, polyvinyl alcohol (PVA), and polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP). The solvent included ethanol, acetone, n-propanol, and propargyl alcohol. The disper- sants used include cyclohexane, pine oil, polyacrylic acid (PAA), and polysulfide styrene (PSS). The Ag NPs with different particle sizes were mixed with organics and mechanically mixed for 12 h to make a homogeneous dispersed paste. The pastes were printed on the surface of the Cu substrate with a stencil thickness of 100 μm. Then, the substrate was placed on a heated platform and preheated at 160 °C for 3 min to solidify the paste. The die (3 × 3 × 1 mm3) was the Ag-coated Cu plate placed on dried Ag NPs. The sandwiched structure was placed on a hot press sintering machine at a temperature of 250 °C with an assisting pressure of 20 MPa for sintering. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Quanta 600 FEG, FEI) was used to observe the morphology of the microstructures on the surface of the laser-ablated samples, and a scanning confocal microscope (LEXT OLS4100, Olympus) was used to observe micro- structure 3D morphology to measure the depth (D) of the micro- structures. The sintered joints were used to determine the shear strength of the joints using a shearing machine (Dage 4000) with a shear rate of 50 μm/s and a shear height of 50 μm. The joints were milled and polished, and SEM will observe the morphology of the interconnect interface (Fig. 1). III. RESULT AND DISCUSSION A. The depth of grooves increased with the increase of laser power and scanning times, because Cu was continuously excited under energy input, and the measure- ments of the groove depth are presented in Table I. The shape of the laser-scanned groove was similar to a “V” character since the focused laser was a Gaussian beam, and the intensity cross-sectional profile of a Gaussian beam at any position on the optical axis is Gaussian. Therefore, the property of the Gaussian beam at the center point had the greatest light intensity, which caused the center of the laser beam to be more effective in removing materials than the surrounding areas. The microscopic morphology of the surface pickled micro- structure is shown in Fig. 3. The surface of Cu presented regular laser-constructed grooves on the surface. These grooves significantly increased the surface area, and surfaces with different groove spacings can be obtained by adjusting the interval of the laser scanning. The smaller the spacing of the grooves, the more surface area was increased. The grooves were filled with ultrafast laser-excited nanoparticles, which could significantly improve the surface energy of the grooves. The formation of the sintered neck required energy, and nanoparticles with high surface energy on the substrate could form a better connection with Ag NPs to improve the connection strength of the interface. The nanoparti- cles were stable on the substrate surface even after ultrasonic pickling. Figure 4 shows the XRD patterns of the substrate surface before and after the laser process.
